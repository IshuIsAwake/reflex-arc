# The team ideation session

Prepared 2026-08-24 for a session that was called off the same day for lack of availability. It has
not happened. Recorded here so it is not re-derived, and moved out of `HANDOFF.md` on 2026-08-25 to
keep that file short.

**Roles are still undivided, and no work-division document should be produced before this runs.**
People who shape a thing do not need to be assigned to it, and the session is the only honest read
on who will actually work.

## The shape: bottom-up, eight beats, the project named only at beat 6

1. Hand on a hot stove.
2. Mars at 3–22 minutes each way — so who stops the rover driving into a hole?
3. Earth's half of the loop is a room full of PhDs, and was never automated.
4. Why an LLM cannot drive and RL cannot decide.
5. Tape floor, ESP32, no brain on board.
6. **Delay the link twenty seconds and it keeps going.**
7. The map is fogged, so the job is deciding what is worth looking at.
8. And it says why, in English.

## Notes on running it

- **Beat 4 is the transcript, not an argument.** [`00-raw-transcript.md`](00-raw-transcript.md)
  262–345 — the model finds the Seal, derives a farm route, is told to go get it, and has no hands.
  Show it rather than describing it.
- **The target is "what if we…", not "wow."** Awe that is not understood produces politeness, and a
  finished design can only be delegated, not joined.
- **Each half must be load-bearing for the other**, or each side files the other under *not my part*
  and stops listening. The latency demo works *because* the rover has no brain; the RL is necessary
  *because* battery cost per slope can only be measured off the real machine. Shared currency:
  battery.
- **Leave the open questions genuinely open** — onboard sensing, day length, grid size, flight
  budget, storm forecast. Roles come out of the session.
- **Hold the onboard-sensing question unclaimed** for the member who could not attend, so she joins
  into ownership rather than into a settled plan.
- Say the hardcoded-table risk out loud. It is what makes this a problem rather than a task.
